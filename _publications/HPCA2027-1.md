## Abstract

Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables LLM inference on encrypted inputs without exposing them to the server. However, deploying FHE LLMs on GPUs faces three bottlenecks: preprocessed plaintexts require tens to hundreds of TiB of storage and are costly to generate online; executing FHE operations individually incurs excessive memory traffic and results in low GPU utilization; and existing multi-GPU schemes suffer from load imbalance and excessive communication.

This paper presents Helm, a multi-GPU system for FHE LLM inference that addresses all three bottlenecks. First, Helm introduces a two-stage plaintext generation method that decouples the expensive encoding pipeline from lightweight layout transformations, enabling efficient on-the-fly data generation from a compact basis set. Second, Helm represents the FHE program as parameterized statements with explicit iteration domains, enabling statement-level operation optimizations, including operation fusion, lazy modular reduction, and operation batching to reduce instruction count and memory traffic and improve GPU utilization. Third, Helm introduces a heuristic multi-GPU partitioning method that uses a cost model to evaluate candidate plans and ILP to select a low-cost plan, accounting for both load balance and inter-GPU communication.

Our evaluation on eight NVIDIA H20 GPUs shows that, for 128-token inputs, Helm outperforms baselines by an average of 26.4× on Llama3-8B and 31.6× on Llama3-70B in terms of Time-To-First-Token (TTFT), while reducing the storage requirement by 96×. Under this setting, Helm achieves a TTFT of 48.8 seconds on Llama3-8B, bringing FHE-based LLM inference one step closer to practical deployment.
