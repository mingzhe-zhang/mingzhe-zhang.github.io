## Abstract

Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) offers unparalleled data security guarantees for privacy-sensitive applications, but its widespread adoption is hindered by prohibitively high computation and memory capacity overheads compared to conventional computation on plaintext data. These inefficiencies create critical performance bottlenecks, limiting FHE’s practicality in real-world scenarios. To enhance performance, we systematically characterize FHE workloads, noting that existing accelerators struggle with state-of-the-art FHE schemes due to bandwidth constraints and the diverse computation patterns of different FHE primitives. In this work, we propose OmniCifro, a heterogeneous FHE acceleration framework that addresses performance bottlenecks through architectural design and dataflow optimization. OmniCifro combines a specialized Homomorphic Processing Unit (HPU) for compute-intensive kernels with Processing-in-Memory (PIM) modules for memory-bound operations, featuring dedicated functional units for polynomial arithmetic and element-wise operations. We further optimize the kernel mapping strategy for advanced FHE algorithms to reduce communication overhead between HPU and PIM. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that OmniCifro achieves a 1.6× performance improvement over ARK.
